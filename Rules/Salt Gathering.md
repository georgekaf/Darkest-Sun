# Salt Gathering

*Prompted by ad6 — the party reached the Salt Sea and discussed harvesting salt to preserve their snake meat, but never actually did it. These rules exist so the next attempt has an answer ready.*

**Draft — needs GM approval before use.** Numbers below are a starting proposal, not settled canon.

Salt is gathered from a salt flat, salt sea shore, or dry saline crust. It is not a wilderness-survival freebie: the crust has to be broken, hauled, and cleaned of grit before it is worth anything.

## Harvesting

- **1 hour of work per character** yields **1 load of raw salt** on a success.
- **DC 12 CON check** — the work is done bent over in reflected glare and full heat. On a failure the hour is spent for nothing.
- Working during the **hottest phases** (late morning through afternoon) imposes **disadvantage**, and counts as strenuous activity for heat/exhaustion purposes.
- Requires a digging or prying implement. Bare-handed gathering is **DC 16** and yields half a load.

## Uses

- **Preserving meat.** 1 load of salt preserves the meat from one large creature (roughly the yield of a giant snake, kank, or erdlu), extending it from spoiling within a day or two to keeping for **2 weeks**. Without salt, butchered meat is worth carrying for one day only.
- **Trade.** Raw salt is low-value bulk — common near the Salt Sea, worth more the further inland it travels. Set price by distance from the nearest salt flat rather than a fixed rate.

## Notes

- Salt from a **salt sea shore** is dirtier than salt from an inland flat; halve the preservation duration unless it is rinsed and dried, which costs another hour.
- Gathering openly on a salt flat is highly visible — there is no cover, and the glare makes the party easy to spot from a distance. Roll for wandering encounters as if travelling, not as if camped.
