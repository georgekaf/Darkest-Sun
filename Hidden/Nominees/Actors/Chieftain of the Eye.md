# Actor: Chieftain of the Eye

*(New actor. Proposed id `actor-eye-chieftain`. **Unnamed in the printed source.** Proposed personal names, if wanted: **Sesskah**, **Nurruk**, **Thagreth**. Id is role-based so a naming ruling costs nothing.)*

## One-Sentence Summary
An ancient gith who sits alone over a glowing stone watching his own ally plot against him, and who has told nobody — because the betrayal is more useful to him as a secret than as an accusation.

## Player-Safe Description

### Appearance
An **ancient** gith, squatting alone over a glowing stone and peering into it. The light from the jewel comes up faintly into his face; inside it can be seen the image of three gith in council.

Over his tent flies a banner displaying a large, terrible eye, which appears to be peering down at the eighty or so gith camped around the standard outside — each of them wrapped in blankets made of reptilian skin.

### Manner and Voice
*(Proposed.)* Still, quiet, and patient in a way the other chieftains find unnerving. The Eye's chieftain listens far more than he speaks, and when he does speak it is with the specific confidence of someone who already knows the answer to the question he is asking.

### Public Reputation
The oldest of the three chieftains and the best equipped — his tribe carries the heaviest armour in the war band. Within the alliance he is regarded as watchful, which is meant as a criticism and is completely accurate.

## Confirmed Facts
- Leads the Tribe of the Eye — roughly 80 gith, encamped around a battle standard bearing a large eye.
- **He is ancient.** The module says so directly, and it is the only chieftain given an age.
- Sits alone inside his tent, staring into a **glowing stone / crystal ball**, in which the image of three gith in conference can be seen.
- **He is spying on the leader of the Hand, listening to his treacherous plans.**
- The Eye's warriors are the most heavily armoured of the three tribes (AR 9, against the Rune's 8 and the Hand's 7), and the slowest (MV 3).
- In the battle plan, **the Eye approaches the side of the temple and attempts to get between the band and the temple door** — a cut-off manoeuvre, not a frontal assault.
- In the final battle, the Rune positions itself in the middle, flanked by the Hand and the Eye.

## Current Activity
Sitting alone in the dark over a glowing stone, watching three gith in another tent plan to rob him, and doing nothing about it.

## GM-Only Secrets
- **The scrying stone is a bigger anomaly than the steel and the module never notices.** Steel weapons can be supplied to a raiding tribe by a patron. A functioning scrying focus, in the hands of an ancient gith who knows how to use it, implies something else — either a second gift from the same patron, or something the Eye found on his own. Given where this campaign goes, a githyanki-made object in a surface gith's tent in Adventure One is a legitimate and very early foreshadow. Recommended: make it match the aesthetic of Yathazor.
- **He is the best interrogation target in the war band.** Ancient, well-informed, cautious, and holding a grievance against an ally. A party that captures him rather than killing him has a source with motive to talk.
- **His inaction is the interesting part.** He has known about the betrayal for some time and has not told the Rune. Play that as calculation: an ally who is about to betray someone else is an ally whose behaviour you can predict, and predictability is worth more to him than justice.
- *(Proposed.)* He expects the war band to fail. The cut-off role he has taken puts his tribe between the enemy and their shelter — which is also the position furthest from the Rune and closest to open desert.

## Proposed Developments
- **Recommended:** pair his tent with the Hand's on the same scouting night, as the module lays them out. Seeing the betrayal being planned *and* seeing it being watched is a much better scene than either alone.
- If the PCs frame the Hand for a raid, the Eye moves fast and hard, because he has been waiting for a reason. That is the cleanest way for the "set the tribes against each other" gambit to pay off.
- The scrying stone is a lootable object with campaign-length implications. If the party takes it, the GM has handed themselves a scrying tool and a githyanki artifact in the same motion.
- If he survives, an ancient gith who saw the alliance collapse is exactly the kind of prisoner Adventure Three's interrogations are built for.

- **Psionics:** contact, id insinuation, mind thrust, life detection, mental barrier.

- **Forewarned:** cannot be surprised by an ally's betrayal, and gains advantage on the first reaction to one.
- **Running him at the table:** he should be discovered mid-scry, lit from below by the stone, watching the scene the party has just come from. That image does the entire characterisation.
