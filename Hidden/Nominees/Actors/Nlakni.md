# Actor: Overseer Nlakni

*(New actor. Proposed id `actor-nlakni`. **Historical figure — millennia dead or long departed. Present only as his sealed quarters.**)*

## One-Sentence Summary
The githyanki who ruled Yathazor from a tower nobody could climb, kept his prisoners in a room they could not plane shift out of, plane shifted away when the city fell, and left behind the only untouched rooms in the entire dead metropolis.

- Subtype: named-npc

- Faction or allegiance: the githyanki of Yathazor; ultimately the lich-queen
- Current location: last known `location-yathazor`, area 2 — the Overseer's Quarters, atop the 400-foot pillar
- Current route, when traveling: teleportation and plane shift
- Role: Overseer — ruler of Yathazor

## Player-Safe Description

### Appearance
Never seen. What survives of Nlakni is a suite of rooms 400 feet in the air that **has not been ruined or stripped** — one of only two such places in Yathazor, the other being the guardhouse.

The interior gives an impression of extreme age. **There are no cobwebs, because the spiders could not reach this area**, but a fine layer of dust covers everything. Many of the less durable items survived only because there is **absolutely no wind or insect life up here to disturb them** — and once the PCs enter, the air currents they set in motion **cause relatively soft items to crumble into dust.**

His armour fits **any thin humanoid approximately 6 feet tall**, which is as close to a physical description as the module gets.

### Manner and Voice
One line survives, cast as a *magic mouth* on the statue in his administrative room. Touch the statue and it says, in githyanki:

> *"Beware, mortal, the wrath of the Queen. I shall bind my enemies and feed them to the Blind Dark."*

**The githyanki tongue sounds horrible and is completely incomprehensible to those who don't speak the language.**

The module notes that this warning **represents some insight that Nlakni had into the mysteries of the lich-queen.**

### Public Reputation
None surviving. Yathazor is not part of the lore of Athas; the city was secret, and **when the multiplanar battle was over, no githyanki remembered Yathazor or how to reach it.**

## Confirmed Facts
- **Overseer Nlakni was the ruler of Yathazor** before it was abandoned. **A 7th/8th-level fighter/mage.**
- His quarters sat atop the Pillar of the Overseer, a 400-foot column in the centre of the city, and served two purposes: **it protected him, because it could only be reached by teleportation or similar means; and it allowed him to watch over Yathazor. His title was quite literal.**
- **He and his trusted aides reached the quarters by teleportation.** The quarters are **lined with lead**, blocking clairvoyance.
- **He placed many traps in his chambers** — fire traps on nearly every door and chest (1d4+8), and a floor-collapse trap in the armoury that drops everyone in the room **400 feet to the street below for 20d6.**
- His administrative room holds a 10-foot marble statue of **the githyanki lich-queen** — a tall, thin, fierce hooded woman holding manacles, **the hood having no eye slits.** Since Nlakni had never seen the lich-queen, and **the queen does not want her image displayed where others can defile it, the statue does not actually look like her.** The significance of the eyeless hood **is known only to the ancient githyanki.**
- He cast the *magic mouth* warning quoted above on that statue.
- His **prison** (area 2) held important prisoners; the same defences that made the quarters hard to penetrate made them hard to escape. It contains the remains of **two githzerai prisoners**, their manacles rusted to nothing, but **two shackles of planar binding survive** — they prevent anyone wearing one from plane shifting. Hidden in each pile of bone fragments is an **amulet of psionic interference**.
- On the pedestal in that room is **an extraplanar prison — a copper hoop — which has held a gray slaad captive since before the fall of Yathazor.** The slaad was an ally of the githzerai.
- A **githzerai** got into his quarters at some point and was killed there; his remains lie beneath a window in the administrative room, with a rusted plate harness and **a two-handed sword +1 still in fine shape.**
- **The Overseer did not have time to grab all his weapons before he plane shifted away**, and some — though not his best ones — remain in the armoury: five **steel-tipped javelins +2**, **plate mail +3, fire resistant** (incredibly gaudy, set with beads and gems, its silk strips now in tatters), and a **shield +1, gaze reflecting.**
- **The tips of those javelins look much like the points on the javelins the gith have been using.** This clue may lead the PCs to suspect the gith found a weapon cache. **In fact, the gith are merely copying the style of the githyanki.**
- His personal quarters hold **538 gold pieces** across eight fire-trapped stone coffers, and behind a secret door: seven rings, numerous beads, three finely wrought bracelets (10,000 cp, up to 15,000 to collectors), **a miniature torture rack made of silver worth 1,000 cp**, and **his spell book** — bound in dry crumbling leather and patina-covered bronze, titled in githyanki *"Belonging to Nlakni"*, with a **sepia snake sigil** beside the title. If read, the sigil strikes at THAC0 13 and immobilises for 1d4+8 days. **The interior pages crumble to dust if the book is opened. The spells are irretrievable.**

## GM-Only Secrets
- **His rooms are the campaign's best single explanation, and the party can skip them entirely.** The module says outright that the PCs can complete the adventure without ever investigating the pillar. What they lose by skipping it: the lich-queen statue, the *Blind Dark* warning, the plane-shift infrastructure, the githzerai corpse, the shackles of planar binding, and **the javelins that prove the gith are copying githyanki design rather than looting a cache.** That last item is the cleanest piece of evidence in the entire campaign.
- **"The Blind Dark is beneath you."** Those words are carved on the ornate archway at the entrance corridor, in githyanki, and the module notes their meaning is unclear because they refer to an obscure mystic aspect of the githyanki's adoration of their lich-queen. Nlakni's *magic mouth* uses the same phrase — *"I shall bind my enemies and feed them to the Blind Dark."* **Two independent references, one dead ruler, no explanation.** This is a campaign-scale hook the module leaves entirely unpulled.
- **He ran.** The Overseer of Yathazor did not die defending it. He plane shifted away without time to collect his best weapons, and **no githyanki afterwards remembered the city or how to reach it.** Whether he survived the war, whether he told anyone, and whether the lich-queen ever learned Yathazor still existed are all open — and Trinth rediscovering it millennia later suggests the answer to the last one is no.
- **The gray slaad in his prison is still alive and is released by any loud noise.** Nlakni's most dangerous legacy is a prisoner who has been in an extraplanar box for thousands of years and is, in the module's phrasing, understandably demented and eager to kill.
- *(Proposed.)* The silver torture rack among his personal valuables is a keepsake, not a tool. It is a miniature.

## Proposed Developments
- **Recommended:** make the javelin comparison land. A PC who has been carrying a captured gith spear since Adventure One, standing in a millennia-old armoury holding an identical githyanki javelin, is the moment the campaign's shape becomes visible.
- The *Blind Dark* is free foreshadowing for Adventure Seven and costs one line each time it appears.
- If the campaign ever wants Nlakni himself — a githyanki Overseer who fled a losing war and has had millennia to become something — the slot is open and completely unconstrained.
- The spell book crumbling is printed and should be honoured. Let them find it, read the title, and lose it.
