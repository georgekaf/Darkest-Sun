# Actor: Arex

*(New actor. Proposed id `actor-arex`. **Thin source — see Sources.**)*

## One-Sentence Summary
The craftsman who told the second meeting tent that artisans go down like sheep in front of a real army — the least popular true thing said in the whole debate.

## Player-Safe Description

### Appearance
*(Proposed.)* An unremarkable artisan, thin, with the stooped shoulders of someone who works seated. Ink or clay under the nails depending on which trade the GM assigns him. He does not look like a man anyone would follow, and he is not trying to be.

### Manner and Voice
*(Proposed, extrapolated from his printed argument.)* Nervous, precise, and unable to stop talking once he has started. Arex makes the case for flight in the manner of a man who has already run the scenario in his head a hundred times and cannot understand why nobody else has.

He uses the word *sheep* about his own people in open council. He does not appear to notice how that lands.

### Public Reputation
Correct and resented. His argument is unanswerable and nobody thanks him for it.

## Confirmed Facts
- A member of Tenpug's Band and one of the minor debaters in the second meeting tent.
- Argues **for flight**, on the grounds that artisans will go down like sheep when confronted by a real army like that of the gith.
- The second tent's conclusion, if the PCs do not intervene, is that since the band knows nothing about fighting it would be best not to try — a conclusion Arex's argument substantially produces.

## Current Activity
Losing an argument he is going to win retroactively, in about a week, at enormous cost.

## GM-Only Secrets
- **He is right, and the module confirms it.** Adventure One is written so that Tenpug's Band is defeated in the first pitched battle regardless of preparation, and is driven back into the temple. Arex predicted precisely this in open council and was ignored.
- **His vindication is the best available scene for the aftermath.** After the retreat, with the hall full of wounded and the doors being battered, Arex is the man everyone now has to look at. The GM should decide whether he says anything. Recommended: he does not.
- *(Proposed.)* He is not a coward — he is the only person in the tent who has correctly imagined the outcome, which looks identical from outside and is the opposite.

## Proposed Developments
- Pair him deliberately with [[Isak]] as the two ends of the same room: the man who says *this is my home* and the man who says *we are not soldiers*. Both are right. That is the actual shape of the debate and it is better than a simple correct answer.
- If the campaign wants a survivor to become bitter and difficult in Adventures Two and Three, Arex is pre-justified — he was right, he was overruled, and people died.
- Alternatively he flees during the first battle, survives, and returns. That is a harder and more interesting version.
