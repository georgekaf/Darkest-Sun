# Actor: Askai

*(New actor. Proposed id `actor-askai`.)*

## One-Sentence Summary
An ancient gith of enormous psionic power who answers a broken war band's plea for help by deciding not to fight its enemy at all — and instead cutting the head off the army by killing the player characters in their sleep.

## Classification
- Subtype: named-npc
- Control: autonomous
- Status: active — **and his end state is a branch point, see GM-Only Secrets**
- Faction or allegiance: `faction-black-spine-gith`
- Current location: `location-nibenay-iron-mine-stockade` (the gith base camp), reaching outward by mind
- Current route, when traveling: does not travel; he does not need to
- Role: Gith lord; psionic warlord

## Player-Safe Description

### Appearance
Not seen. Askai is experienced almost entirely as landscape and weather: a sky gone deep red, sand turned black as midnight, twin violet moons, and a rising wind full of screaming.

The things the party actually looks at are all him wearing something else — a shapeless horror hunting the sleeping ranks, two iron pillars that unfold into gith-shaped giants of living metal, a twelve-foot gith lord in gleaming chitin with a steel axe and a terrible grin.

His actual body is an old gith sitting somewhere underground, and by the time the party sees where it is, they are inside his head rather than in a room with him.

### Manner and Voice
Silent. Askai never speaks to the PCs, never negotiates, and never announces himself. Every communication is an assault.

This is characterisation rather than absence: he does not regard the party as parties to a conversation. They are an obstacle with a head, and he is removing the head.

### Public Reputation
None. Nobody on the surface of Athas knows he exists until the night the whole army fails to wake up.

## Confirmed Facts
- An **ancient gith with great psionic powers**, at the gith base camp.
- The commander of the Rune, his war band broken, sent a messenger asking for help. Askai answered the plea.
- His method, stated in the text: *in order to stop this army of freed slaves, he will simply cut off its head. With the head gone, the army will lose its direction and disintegrate.* The head is the player characters.
- The psychic assault begins the day before the army reaches the gith camp.
- **First night — the testing attack.** One PC wakes to find the entire army asleep, including the sentries, under a red sky on black sand. A storm rises; something hunts victims out of the ranks. This is a *persona* of Askai — his ego given a shape. It kills nearly thirty people before it can be found.
- Those killed in the dream do not wake. They are not dead; they are comatose, must be carried, and slow the army to six miles a day.
- **Thri-kreen are unaffected**, because they do not sleep.
- **Second night — the full assault.** He sends his servant, the imp [[Kalisore]], to place a magical stone in the middle of the army to properly complete the spell. Once the stone is placed, even thri-kreen must save or fall unconscious.
- If any thri-kreen spots the imp flying in, or picks up the stone, **the spell is ruined** and all the sleepers can be woken normally.
- In the full dream, nothing the PCs do works. Askai is in complete control of the dream reality; the printed instruction is that it is not their dream, it is his. An undead army rides them down and the dream ends with the PCs making their final stand.
- The magical stone is left behind regardless of whether the imp was caught, and the PCs will find it.
- **The stone is his own weapon turned around.** Anyone with the psionic ability of *contact* can use it, in conjunction with others, to reach the stone's owner — and thereby enter the dreamscape deliberately and go hunting for him.
- His psionic defences, encountered in that order: **two Iron Sentinels** (personifications of his *tower of iron will*), a **twelve-foot Giant Gith** in chitinous armour with a steel axe, and a third horror that is pure bluff — it vanishes the first time it is struck.
- **If the party flees the third creature, everything vanishes, the dream stone is broken and unusable, and he has severed contact.**
- Beyond the defences is a fortress shaped like a giant gith skull, rising from black sand, encrusted with tiny glowing jewels. A large jewel floats at its centre with dark shapes moving inside it — the army's trapped minds.
- **Breaking the centre stone alone wakes the army and drives Askai insane. Breaking the other stones first kills him.** The choice is made by the party, usually without understanding what they are choosing.

## Traits and Pressures
- Ambition: High and unhurried.
- Caution: Very high (5) — he attacks from a position where he cannot be reached, until the party works out how to reach it.
- Secrecy: 5. He is not identified by name until Adventure Two, Part Four, well after he has begun killing people.
- Loyalty: To the gith, and above them to whatever the Rune's banner points at.
- Cruelty: Total, and cold rather than gleeful. He does not torment; he processes.
- Risk tolerance: Very low. Everything he does is at a distance, through proxies, in a medium he controls absolutely.
- Safety: Effectively perfect, until the exact moment it is not.
- Wealth: Irrelevant to him.
- Status: The most powerful gith the campaign has produced so far, and answerable to something further down.

## Resources and Capabilities
- Psionic reach sufficient to blanket an entire marching army at a distance of miles.
- Total authorship of a dream reality — the module states flatly that nothing the PCs attempt inside it works differently than it would in life, because it is his dream.
- A persona: his ego given independent murderous form (AC 2, 8 HD, 40 hp, two attacks at 1d10, **immune to psionics**, movement 24).
- Layered mental defences: two Iron Sentinels (AC 0, 15 HD, 80 hp, 4d8 damage), a Giant Gith (AC −5, 12 HD, 65 hp, 1d10+4), and one bluff.
- The imp [[Kalisore]] as a physical-world servant.
- The anchor stone — small, black, uncut, a dark light in the middle, radiating magic and **indestructible by normal means**.

## Current Activity

## GM-Only Secrets
- **His defeat is a branching state and it changes Adventure Two's finale.** Printed, explicitly:
  - **Killed** (other stones broken first) → a replacement assumes command, and the fort at the mine is *well defended*. The party has a hard fight.
  - **Driven insane** (centre stone only) → the gith are scattered and poorly organised, morale is low, and any concerted attack routs them.
  - The party will almost never understand that they are making this choice. Recommended: let them make it blind, and let the consequence arrive two sessions later without comment.
- **The third dream-guardian is a bluff and the party can lose to it by winning the argument with themselves.** It vanishes on the first hit — but if *all* of them run, the dream stone breaks permanently and Askai has severed contact for good. The scariest-looking thing in the sequence is the only one that cannot hurt them, and fleeing it is the single worst outcome available. Describe it as horribly as possible; that is printed instruction.
- **He is beatable only with his own equipment.** The party's route into his mind is the anchor stone his own servant planted. If the thri-kreen catches the imp too early, the module tells the GM to skip ahead — the attack simply ends, and the party never learns who did it or how close they came.
- *(Proposed.)* The comatose are not incidental. He takes minds and holds them in a jewel; he does not simply kill. What the gith intend to do with thirty stored minds is unaddressed, and given where the campaign goes — a githyanki queen harvesting Athasian psionics — it is not a coincidence worth wasting.

## Proposed Developments
- **Recommended:** name him early. The module withholds "Askai" until Part Four, long after the horror starts. A single overheard gith word, or a name surfacing in the dream, makes the second night personal.
- If the party has [[Ch'k-Chakal]] with them, the imp scene is his. If they left him outside the gith patrol screen in Adventure One, they will not have a thri-kreen and the full assault lands. That is a genuine two-adventure consequence and it should be honoured.
- The stored-minds thread connects directly to Trinth's stated goal in Adventure Seven. Making Askai's jewel visibly the same technology as the Nightmare Gate's harvest is nearly free.
- If driven insane rather than killed, he is available later as a broken thing the party finds, which is a better scene than a corpse.

## Stat Block or Rules Notes
- Class: Psionicist [UC]
- Level: 10
- Armor Class: 12
- Hit Points: 44 — **and largely irrelevant; see below**
- Movement: near
- Strength +0, Dexterity +1, Constitution +2, Intelligence +4, Wisdom +4, Charisma +2
- Alignment: Chaotic (evil)
- Morale: 12
- Attacks: 1 attack per round in the physical world, which he never uses.
- **Unreachable:** Askai cannot be attacked physically during Adventure Two. He is miles away. The entire encounter takes place inside his mind, and the party's stat block matters far more than his does.
- **Dreamlord:** within his dreamscape he sets all conditions. Player actions that would alter reality do not work; the module is explicit that nothing they attempt works differently than it would in life.
- **Persona** — his hunting shape: AC 14, HP 40, 2 attacks at +6 for 1d10, movement double near, **immune to psionics**. Attacks against it in the storm suffer disadvantage; it suffers none.
- **Iron Sentinels** (×2) — his *tower of iron will* personified: AC 17, HP 80, +8 for 4d8.
- **Giant Gith** — twelve feet, chitin, steel axe: AC 18, HP 65, +9 for 1d10+4.
- **The Third Thing** — AC —, HP —. **Vanishes on the first successful hit or spell.** Cannot deal damage. Its only weapon is the description.
- **The Skull Fortress:** breaking the central jewel frees the army and shatters his mind. Breaking peripheral jewels first kills him outright. One or the other, chosen by the players, usually unknowingly.
- **Running him at the table:** he is weather, then a monster, then a fortress, and only ever a person in retrospect. Do not give him dialogue. The horror is that the party is being killed by something that has not addressed them.
- **Conversion note:** the module gives Askai **no personal stat block at all** — only his dream constructs. The dream creatures above are converted from their printed 2e lines. The psionicist block is constructed to support "an ancient gith with great psionic powers."
