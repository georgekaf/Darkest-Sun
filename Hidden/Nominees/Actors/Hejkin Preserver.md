# Actor: Hejkin Preserver

*(New actor. Proposed id `actor-hejkin-preserver`. **Unnamed in the printed source.** She is a distinct individual with a stat block, spells, tactics and dialogue behaviour, so she is filed as an actor rather than a monster entry. Proposed personal names, if wanted: **Grekka**, **Ulmet**, **Sorrn**.)*

## One-Sentence Summary
The invisible hejkin wizard who walks down the middle of a mine tunnel with fourteen warriors phased into the rock on either side of her, taps twice, and kills whatever is between them — and who hates the gith considerably more than she hates you.

## Player-Safe Description

### Appearance
Short, thick and misshapen, with **enormous claws in place of fingers**. Hejkin are subterranean, stone-dwelling, and profoundly ugly by surface standards.

The party will almost certainly not see her at all. She casts *invisibility* on herself before contact and walks the centre of the tunnel; when she is spotted, she retreats completely into the rock and leaves only vibrations.

Her warriors do not appear until the walls come alive on both sides at once.

### Manner and Voice
No shared language. **The hejkin tongue is extremely difficult for surface dwellers and is very rarely heard above ground.** Without magic or psionics there is no conversation.

If the PCs try to communicate anyway, a hejkin will attempt psionic contact using *send thoughts* — and because verbal thought cannot cross the language barrier, **it sends images the PC has to interpret**. That is the character's actual voice: pictures, pushed into your head, by something that despises what you are.

### Public Reputation
None on the surface. Underground, her clan are the ones who kill miners.

## Confirmed Facts
- **Hejkin, 4th-level preserver, female.** She leads a band of **14 hejkin warriors**.
- The band travelled to the mines to investigate recent activity.
- **They hate everyone who defiles the earth — that is, anyone who digs mines — so they are enemies of both the Nibenese miners and the gith.**
- Their combat experience is mostly against gith and 0-level miners, so they expect to defeat most groups they meet. **They are wrong about this party and the fight turns quickly.**
- Tactics: she casts *invisibility* on herself and walks down the centre of the tunnel; seven hejkin phase through the rock on either side of her. She taps the right-hand wall with her claws to signal them to stop and prepare, taps the left the same way, then phases in until only her invisible head protrudes.
- The tapping is audible **only** if the party is approaching silently or has an exceptional way to detect sound.
- When the party is between the two groups, she touches the warrior nearest her to signal the attack, and the walls come alive on both sides.
- If she can tell she has been spotted, **she retreats completely into the rock** and uses *heightened senses* to hear — or feel the vibrations of — when the party is in a good position, then ambushes anyway.
- When the tide turns, the hejkin **phase through the rock to escape**. Quick-thinking PCs may be able to capture one or two.
- **Captured warriors may be persuaded to help.** As much damage as the Nibenese miners have done, the injuries caused by the gith have been much greater, and the hejkin's grudge against the gith goes back much further. A hejkin can point the party toward Zigath's Nest.
- A convinced hejkin warrior might scout for them using its phasing ability — but must make a morale check after each mission or go home, and **will abandon the party when they find the City of Calamity**, at which point things become too strange and frightening.
- **If the preserver herself is captured**, she can tell them about the nearby gith tunnels and about the "City of Calamity" as the hejkin call it, which she describes simply as *"a deadly, unnatural area where only the foolish tread."*
- **She will not serve as a scout, though she may pretend to in order to escape.** She is too valuable to her clan to risk, and she phases away at the first opportunity.
- The PCs may try to persuade a captured hejkin to rally its fellows for a combined assault on the gith. **Under no circumstance will such an attack come about.** Even if convinced, its fellows refuse — they would rather the two kinds of "mountain-killers" destroy each other without hejkin risking their lives.
- She carries a sack of ore chunks worth 1d10+10 cp and **a large red unpolished garnet worth 50 cp**. Hejkin do not cut or polish gems, considering altered stones to be in bad taste.

## Current Activity
Invisible, phased into a tunnel wall, waiting for the party to walk between her two files of warriors.

## GM-Only Secrets
- **This encounter is a test of whether the party takes prisoners, and the module says so.** The hejkin are outclassed and the real question is whether quick-thinking PCs can see potential allies rather than corpses. A party that slaughters all fifteen loses the campaign's only friendly guide to the tunnels and the first mention of Yathazor.
- **The name "City of Calamity" enters the campaign here, from a captive.** It is the first the party hears of Yathazor, and it arrives as a warning from something that lives in the rock and is frightened of it. That framing is worth protecting — do not let the players learn the name any other way first.
- **She is a negotiator who intends to defect.** She may pretend to serve as a scout purely to get an opportunity to phase away. A party that trusts the preserver rather than a warrior gets nothing. A party that keeps a *warrior* gets a real, if flaky, scout.
- **The alliance the players will inevitably propose cannot happen.** The module forecloses it explicitly. Let them try; let the refusal be reasoned rather than arbitrary. *"Let the mountain-killers kill each other"* is a coherent position and the party is, from the hejkin's view, one of the two kinds of mountain-killer.
- *(Proposed.)* She is right about them. The party is in the mountain to fight over a mine.

## Proposed Developments
- **Recommended:** run the ambush at full lethality, then make the surrender/capture window obvious. The encounter's value is entirely on the far side of the fight.
- If a hejkin warrior scouts for them, use the printed morale mechanic — check after each mission, plus the talking PC's reaction adjustment — and honour the abandonment at Yathazor. A guide who quits at the threshold of the strange place is better than one who follows.
- The Slate connection is free and unexploited: two parties living in the same mountain, one of whom has explicitly considered and dismissed the other. Worth one line of dialogue in Adventure Four.
- If the campaign wants an underground faction later, the hejkin clan is pre-motivated, pre-hostile to the gith, and pre-unwilling — which makes them interesting rather than convenient.

## Stat Block or Rules Notes
- Class: Wizard (preserver)
- Level: 4
- Armor Class: 12 — **effectively far higher; she is invisible and inside a wall**
- Hit Points: 12
- Movement: near (slow) — **and phases through stone at the same rate**
- Strength +0, Dexterity +1, Constitution +1, Intelligence +2, Wisdom +2, Charisma +0
- Alignment: Neutral (evil)
- Morale: 11
- Attacks: 2 attacks per round.
  - *Claws* +3, 1d4 (×2)
- **Phasing:** can move through solid rock freely, and can remain within it indefinitely, including with only part of the body protruding.
- **Preserver spells:** charm person, sleep, shield; invisibility (**already cast**), web.
- **Psionics:** life draining; biofeedback, body equilibrium, contact, heightened senses, post-hypnotic suggestion, **send thoughts**. *(Printed note: hejkin can use defence modes even while in combat.)*
- **Send Thoughts:** her only communication channel with the party. Transmits **images, not words** — the language barrier blocks verbal thought.
- **Heightened Senses:** can locate the party by sound or by the vibration of their footfalls, even when they are silent, from inside the rock.
- **Warriors (14):** AC 12, HP 8 each, 2 claw attacks at +3 for 1d4, phasing, same psionic devotion list, morale 10. Each carries a quarter-pound sack of ore chunks worth 1d10+10 cp.
- **Running her at the table:** the ambush should feel unfair, because it is — two walls, no warning, and a leader nobody can see. Then it should collapse fast, because they badly misjudged the party. Everything worth having is in what happens next.
- **Conversion note:** printed 2e stats are Hejkin 4th-Level Preserver, Int 14, AL NE, AC 10, MV 6, HD 4d4, hp 7, THAC0 17, 2 attacks 1d4/1d4, ML 11, XP 420, PSPs 85. Hit points raised from 7 to 12 so she survives long enough to be captured, which is the point of the encounter.
