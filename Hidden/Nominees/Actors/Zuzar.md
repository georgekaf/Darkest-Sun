# Actor: Overlord Zuzar

*(New actor. Proposed id `actor-zuzar`.)*

## One-Sentence Summary
The gith Overlord running the deep mine — a competent administrator who has met every impossible deadline she was given, has just been told her life is forfeit if she misses the next one, and cannot escape through the only exit because it will not let her pass.

## Player-Safe Description

### Appearance
A gith of exceptional intelligence, holding a stone-and-adobe building **ornate with crude drawings and hieroglyphs on all sides, with no windows or doors visible** — access is by a freestanding stair to a single circular opening in the roof, and a ladder down.

She carries a steel-tipped spear, a **scarab of enraging enemies** and a **gem of brilliance**.

### Manner and Voice
*(Manner proposed; the situation is printed.)* Precise and controlled, and currently operating under a degree of pressure that would break most commanders. Zuzar does not bluster. She explains, in order, what she was asked to do and what she delivered — and it did not help.

**She will negotiate with the player characters, but only until she feels her troops are in position to overrun them.**

### Public Reputation
Within the mine: the Overlord. Above her: a subordinate who has just been publicly accused of insubordination in front of her own officers.

## Confirmed Facts
- **Gith Overlord Zuzar**, female, exceptional Intelligence — the highest-Intelligence gith the party has met. 80 PSPs.
- She is **the gith overseer of the mine**.
- **She has just finished conducting a tour for two visiting githyanki commanders.**
- **They accused her of insubordination for failing to maintain a steady supply of iron for the construction of their Nightmare Gate.**
- **She explained that she has met every deadline given to her, and that as each one was met a more impossible schedule took its place.**
- **Now, in the final hours of the Nightmare Gate's preparation, she has been commanded to work her slaves to death in order to meet demand.**
- **The githyanki commanders departed through the arch at area 26, swearing that Zuzar's life would be forfeit if she failed in this task.**
- **She is currently meeting with her three top officers** to decide how the slaves can best be used in their final hours.
- **She knows the arch (area 26) is impassable to her, leaving her no means of escape if the plans of her superiors fail.**
- **She also knows that any delay in the mining operations will slow the opening of the Nightmare Gate — a delay that would certainly mean her death.**
- **If she is aware of the PCs, she will recognise that they pose a great threat to her plans and must be destroyed as quickly as possible.** She will **divert as many of her troops to this task as possible, leaving the slaves rather poorly guarded** — and if the PCs notice, **they might take advantage of the situation to lead a slave revolt.**
- **Should the PCs create a commotion and let their presence be known, a guard immediately informs the Overlord.** She rallies as many guards as possible into a defensive position and **will not hesitate to use slaves as a shield wall or hostages.**
- Magical items: **scarab of enraging enemies**, **gem of brilliance**.
- Three **gith lieutenants** attend her.

## GM-Only Secrets
- **She is a cornered mid-level manager and that is the most interesting thing about her.** She met every deadline. Each success bought a worse schedule. She has now been told, in front of her officers, that failure means death — and the door her accusers left through **will not let her follow.** The module gives a gith antagonist a genuinely sympathetic bind and then never uses it.
- **Which raises the obvious play: she is negotiable, and the module half-says so.** She *will* negotiate — but "only until she feels her troops are in a position to overrun them." That is a commander bargaining from weakness while pretending otherwise. **A party that offers her something better than certain death has leverage nobody else in the campaign has offered her.**
- **The arch is one-way by design and she is on the wrong side of it.** The Gray Arch admits only githyanki, gith, and those in their company — yet she describes it as impassable *to her*. Read literally, her own superiors have sealed her in. Whether that is deliberate containment or she simply cannot pass without a githyanki escort is unresolved and is worth deciding, because it determines whether she is a jailer or an inmate.
- **The slave revolt is signposted.** If she diverts her guards onto the PCs, the pens are left poorly guarded, and the module explicitly invites the party to notice. There are 38 slaves in the pits, 30 in the quarry, more in the mine. **This is the intended solution to a facility the party cannot beat by force.**
- *(Proposed.)* She has already decided the quota is impossible and is now managing her own death rather than the mine.

## Proposed Developments
- **Recommended:** let the party overhear the aftermath of the inspection rather than meeting her cold. A gith Overlord explaining to three officers that they have all been sentenced is a far better introduction than a stat block behind a door.
- Play the negotiation for real. If the PCs can offer her passage out of the mountain, or the destruction of the Gate she is being killed over, she has every reason to listen and no reason to trust them.
- If they kill her, the mine's command collapses and the revolt becomes trivial. If they turn her, they gain the only guide to the Gray Arch and what lies beyond it.
- Whether Raskon was one of the two inspecting commanders is a free connection that makes Adventure Six's antagonist real two adventures early.
