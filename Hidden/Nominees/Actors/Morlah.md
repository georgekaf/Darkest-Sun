# Actor: Morlah

*(New actor. Proposed id `actor-morlah`.)*

## One-Sentence Summary
A young bard whose mind Slate broke, who lost every one of his abilities except the tunes, and who now wanders a dead githyanki city drumming on walls with bone clubs and singing a nonsense rhyme that happens to be an accurate prophecy.

## Classification
- Subtype: named-npc
- Control: autonomous
- Status: active
- Faction or allegiance: **hangs around the Square of Gurdek but is not a member**
- Current location: `location-yathazor` — wandering, usually near the Refuge
- Current route, when traveling: no route; he goes where the surfaces are interesting to hit
- Role: The Square's other failure; the party's first contact

## Player-Safe Description

### Appearance
**At first he may seem to be a bard who is down on his luck.** His clothes are the dirty, tattered remnants of the brightly coloured and gaudy outfit he once wore, **but one can still recognise that they were a performer's costume.**

He is **young, with boyish good looks and short sandy-blond hair.** He carries **three bone clubs at all times**, which double as drumsticks.

### Manner and Voice
**He walks and gestures as a sane man would.** That is the trap.

**When he speaks, only nonsense comes forth, punctuated with hauntingly beautiful snatches of songs tossed together at random.** He is not singing in a foreign tongue — a listener will assume he is, and he is simply babbling.

**His expression runs the gamut of human emotions, from idiot friendliness to blinding terror to unholy rage to blissful ignorance**, and it does so without warning or apparent cause.

Persuaded to talk about the city or Slate's plan, he sings this — roughly translated out of his nonsense:

> *The day of stone will break your bone.*
> *Sing hey diddle doddly do.*
> *The day of earth shall be your curse.*
> *Sing hey diddle doodly dee.*
> *The fire dies, and wind just sighs.*
> *Sing hey diddle doodly day.*
> *You think it bad, but it is worse.*
> *Sing hey diddle doddly die.*

### Public Reputation
None. He is a noise in a dead city that nobody knows exists.

## Confirmed Facts
- **Human male, formerly a 5th-level bard**, chaotic good — **and insane**. Intelligence 7, Wisdom 4, Charisma 12.
- **Another of Slate's failures**, driven mad by a failed initiation.
- **Without the intelligence, charisma and neutral viewpoint that bards need, Morlah has lost his bard abilities.** He retains his hit points and THAC0, **and all that is left of his former abilities are snatches of tunes and songs.**
- **He hangs around the Square of Gurdek, but he is not a member.** Because he has been touched by Gurdek, **Slate keeps him fed.**
- He spends most of his time wandering near the Square, entertaining himself by singing and **pounding on different surfaces with his bone clubs.**
- He cannot *ignore earth*; **Ari Evan had to *teleport other* him into Yathazor**, along with Olton.
- **He is the party's first contact with the Square.** An encounter with Morlah draws the group into contact with them, and **how the PCs deal with the crazy bard determines how the Square reacts to them.**
- The encounter opens with an erratic thumping like an insane drum, accompanied by a beautiful singing voice in an unrecognisable language. **It is easy for the PCs to track down: Morlah pounding on a wall.**
- **The PCs are not the only ones who hear it.** Roll on the random encounter table; creatures likely to investigate arrive 1d10 rounds after the party meets him.
- **If the PCs are friendly, he invites them back to the Refuge** — though the invitation is garbled and they will not be sure what to expect.
- **If they threaten him, he tries to slip away and get back to the Refuge, and the group can easily follow.**
- **If they kill or capture him, he cannot lead them back**, and they will have to explore for considerably longer before finding the Refuge.
- He also appears on the wandering-encounter table for Yathazor. If it is too early for the PCs to meet him, **he may flee at first sight — or they may simply hear the drumming and fail to find its source.**
- Resistant to mind reading, like everyone Gurdek touched.

## Current Activity
Wandering Yathazor's Square of Gurdek, singing the same rhyme to anyone who will listen, and being ignored by everyone who lives there.

## GM-Only Secrets
- **The song is correct.** Read it as what it is rather than as flavour: *the day of stone will break your bone* — the Square; *the day of earth shall be your curse* — the earth clerics who dug the tunnel that lets the gith through; *the fire dies, and wind just sighs* — the other elements have already lost; *you think it bad, but it is worse* — the githyanki behind the gith. A broken bard is the only creature in Yathazor telling the party the truth, and he is doing it in nonsense verse with a nursery rhyme refrain.
- **He is a test, and the module says so outright.** How the party treats a harmless madman determines how the Square receives them. Kindness gets them an invitation. Threats get them followed home anyway. Killing him costs them the shortcut. **This is the only social check in Adventure Four that the players do not know they are taking.**
- **The drumming is dangerous.** It draws wandering monsters on a printed timer. A party that stands around interrogating him in the open in Yathazor has made a mistake, and the module will collect on it 1d10 rounds later.
- **He is the reason to run the Refuge as hospitality rather than combat.** If the PCs were decent to him, the Square opens with food and conversation, and the horror is retrospective. If they were not, it opens with a prepared ambush.
- *(Proposed.)* He was good. Charisma 12 is what is left of a performer who played well enough for Slate to select him for wisdom, and the beautiful snatches are the residue of real skill.

## Proposed Developments
- **Recommended:** run the first-contact scene at full length and let the players choose. Do not signal that it is a test.
- The song should be delivered once, in full, and never explained. If a player writes it down and works it out four adventures later, that is the payoff.
- If he survives the Refuge, he is a good, sad recurring presence in Yathazor — the party keeps hearing drumming as they move through the city.
- If the campaign wants one moment of grace, a PC with bardic ability getting a single lucid line out of him would be worth it, and should cost something.
