# Actor: Durdon

*(New actor. Proposed id `actor-durdon`. **⚠ Overlaps an existing sandbox actor — see the Duplication notice.**)*

## ⚠ Duplication notice

`actors/orruk-vesh.json` already exists in mk-sandbox: *"Wounded dwarf miner and survivor of the gith attack… Orruk knows the mine's passages, the fate of missing workers, and where the gith first brought their metal weapons underground."* Status `recovering`, condition `injured`, located at `location-nibenay-iron-mine-stockade`, `canonStatus: mixed-official-and-project-description`.

That is Durdon's role, filled by a campaign-original character. Three resolutions, in order of preference:

1. **They are the same dwarf.** Adopt `actor-orruk-vesh` as canonical and treat "Durdon" as the printed name superseded by campaign use — exactly the pattern already applied to Rakskon→Raxxon. This file then becomes an update to Orruk Vesh, and the escape-by-feigned-death and the Reclamation intelligence attach to him.
2. **They are two different miners.** Orruk survived at the stockade above; Durdon escaped separately, deeper in the tunnels. Both are wounded dwarf miners with knowledge of the passages, which is redundant but not contradictory.
3. **Drop Durdon** and give his Adventure Three scene to Orruk Vesh.

**Option 1 is recommended** and is assumed nowhere in this file — the content below is written so it can be attached to either name.

A dwarf miner who murdered his own death — faked a severed artery with a psionic trick, lay still while the gith marched past his "corpse," and then crawled down a side tunnel to bleed quietly and be found by tembo instead.

## Player-Safe Description

### Appearance
A dwarf miner in a bad way — recently and badly wounded, wounds crudely self-tended, caked in his own dried blood and rock dust. There is a dried blood stain at the mouth of the side tunnel he crawled down, which is what leads searchers to him.

Eight hit points on a good day. He currently has two.

### Manner and Voice
Voluble, profane, and completely undeterred. Durdon talks fast and swears by earth and stone, and he does not seem to have registered that he nearly died. He recounts his escape as a good story rather than a trauma, and he recounts what the gith said in front of him with total recall.

He wants to come with you. He is very insistent about this.

### Public Reputation
None. He is one of the mine's slaves, presumed dead by the gith who left him.

## Confirmed Facts
- A dwarf miner, among **the last of the miners taken into the gith tunnels**.
- Did not fear death at the hands of the gith; his desire was to reach freedom.
- While being marched through the mine he took an extraordinary risk: **he struck a gith guard**, and when the guard struck back he used his **wild psionic talent for biofeedback** to create an impressive splash of blood.
- Convinced they had hit an artery and killed him, the gith left the "corpse" lying at the mouth of a side tunnel.
- Durdon then **used biofeedback to stop the bleeding**, feigned death until the gith had gone, crawled down the side tunnel, and tended his wounds.
- His plan would have worked but for **six tembo** that entered the mine to escape the heat and lie in wait for easy prey.
- When the PCs hear his scream, the tembo have found him and are terrorising him for fun before killing him. **They will kill him in three rounds unless the PCs intervene.**
- He stops screaming once the surprise is over, so the party must stop at branches and listen to find him.
- If rescued, he reports intelligence the surface gith never gave: the gith spoke of **"our new queen"** — vaguely, as though the speaker did not know her name — of everyone becoming her slaves, and of a **"super-lord gith warrior" coming to join the Reclamation**.
- **"The Reclamation"** is what the gith call the attack. It refers to their belief that they once ruled Athas and are merely retaking what is rightfully theirs.
- Durdon does not know it, but he is referring to the **lich-queen of the githyanki** and to **[[Haza]]**.
- He volunteers to accompany the party — *"By earth and stone, I'll smash their heads with my bare hands if you won't lend me a weapon. They've got my buddy, [[Tormar]]."*
- **Allowing him to come would be a terrible mistake.** He has no skill with weapons and is likely to be killed in the next encounter. The module instructs the GM that thoughtful PCs should refuse *and give him a good, face-saving reason not to follow* — for instance, that they need him to carry what he heard back to the mining camp.

## GM-Only Secrets
- **He is a three-round timer, and the party does not know it.** They hear a scream, and then silence, and then they have to search branches while a clock they cannot see runs down. This is one of the module's better-designed openings and it depends entirely on the GM actually running the timer.
- **He is the campaign's exposition delivery, and he is disposable.** Everything the party learns about the Reclamation, the queen and Haza comes from a two-hit-point dwarf who dies to a pack of tembo if they are slow. If he dies, that intelligence simply does not enter the campaign until much later and much harder.
- **The face-saving refusal is the actual roleplay challenge.** The module explicitly asks the GM to make thoughtful PCs invent a dignified reason to send a wounded man away from his friend. Do not let them just say no. Make them find the line.
- *(Proposed.)* He knows Tormar is probably already dead. He is not going to say so, and he is not going to stop.

## Proposed Developments
- **Recommended: adopt option 1** from the Duplication notice and merge him into [[Orruk Vesh]]. The campaign already has a wounded dwarf mine survivor with exactly this knowledge; giving him Durdon's escape story and Reclamation intelligence enriches an existing actor instead of adding a redundant one.
- If the party sends him back to the camp with the intelligence, that is a clean handoff and he can survive the campaign.
- If they take him along, honour the module: he dies in the hejkin ambush or at Zigath's Nest, and it is their fault, and Tormar is still down there.
- Tormar's fate is a free hook of whatever size the campaign wants — a body, a slave in the pens, or something worse in the cold smelter.
