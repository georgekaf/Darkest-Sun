## GM-Only Secrets
- **This is the single best small scene in Adventure Five and it is written as set dressing.** A guard so entertained by the torment of his own workers that he has his back to the room; a fire elemental so bored by captivity that it plays with its food; and three people who cannot move their feet, trying not to falter. **The whole moral architecture of the gith mine is in one fire pit.**
- **The guard's back is turned and the module says so.** He is *oblivious to almost everything* except the fire pit. This is an explicit stealth opening and the party should be given the chance to notice it.
- **Freeing the elemental is a trap that isn't one.** Players will assume a released fire elemental turns on them. **It does not** — it leaves immediately for its own plane, because it has been gone too long. A party that frees it removes the guard's amusement, the slaves' torment and a hazard, and pays nothing.
- **The morale check is the real content.** *Each* of the three rolls separately to decide whether to help the PCs or run for the stairs. **Play them as individuals for that one moment** — one stays, one runs, one hesitates — and they stop being a stat line. It is the cheapest characterisation in the adventure and the module hands it over for free.
- *(Proposed.)* Their feet are in stone because they are cooks working near an exit, not because they are dangerous. Somebody decided that immobilising three people permanently was cheaper than watching them.

## Proposed Developments
- **Recommended:** run the fire pit as a scene, not a room description. Let the party watch it for a round before deciding anything.
- Getting the stone off their feet is a real problem with several answers — a hammer, a stone-shaping spell, an earth cleric, or Quixoth's *strength*. Make them solve it.
- Use the individual morale results. If one runs and dies on the stairs, that lands.
- If the campaign wants Tormar found alive, this detail is a plausible place, and it would make the trio's names matter retroactively.
