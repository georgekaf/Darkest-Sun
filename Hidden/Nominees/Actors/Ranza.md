# Actor: Ranza

*(New actor. Proposed id `actor-ranza`.)*

## One-Sentence Summary
A 12th-level thief who traded a wealthy, successful career for a 2nd-level clerical vocation he cannot remember choosing, and who is now deliberately, miserably clumsy — the designed weak point in the Square of Gurdek.

## Classification
- Subtype: named-npc
- Control: autonomous
- Status: active
- Faction or allegiance: the Square of Gurdek — junior member
- Current location: `location-yathazor` — the Refuge, area 4
- Current route, when traveling: perimeter sweeps around the Refuge
- Role: Earth cleric (nominal); the Square's rogue (actual)

## Player-Safe Description

### Appearance
Thin, short and unkempt. **His face carries an intricate design of black tattoos.** He looks gawky and ill at ease, and moves badly — which is a performance, and an exhausting one.

Leather armour +1, a sling, a steel long sword, a steel dagger, and a **wooden dagger** he has no obvious use for.

### Manner and Voice
**Sour to glum, and never anything else.** Ranza is in a perpetual bad mood he cannot account for and will not examine.

He talks about Slate with genuine, uncomplicated admiration — *"The man's a visionary, I tell you… he's bringing it back to life"* — and about the gith with a studied blankness: *"I try not to get involved with politics. Not my game, no sir. People can fight and kill each other if they want. I'm a holy man, now. Count me a neutral."*

### Public Reputation
Within the Square: the low-level cleric, a little old for such a lowly position. Nobody there thinks about him much. That is the whole problem.

## Confirmed Facts
- **Human male, 12th-level thief / 2nd-level earth cleric**, chaotic neutral. Dexterity 18, Wisdom 17.
- Was an **experienced, highly successful and wealthy thief** to whom walking silently and delicately was instinct.
- To adopt his clerical studies he has had to **put his thief abilities out of his mind** — consciously giving up the smooth, stealthy step he perfected over years of practice. **He is intentionally clumsy.**
- He is **endlessly upset about the loss of his thieving career** and **cannot voice this resentment, even to himself.** He just seems to be in a permanently bad mood.
- Ideally he should forget his thief skills until 13th level as a cleric, **but the Square needs his rogue abilities often enough that he is progressing very slowly as a cleric.**
- If the PCs meet him on friendly terms, **he seems to be a low-level cleric.** Once trouble starts, **he happily switches to his old abilities and becomes, in essence, a thief who can cast a few cleric spells.**
- **Ranza is the weak spot in the Square.** Somewhere in the back of his mind he knows he has plummeted from wealth and success to a lowly earth cleric with no status and no money.
- **If the PCs identify him as the least loyal member, they may be able to get favours from him — ranging from helpful hints to outright betrayal of the Square.**
- When the Square receives guests, **Ranza stays out of sight**, making sure no enemies sneak up on the Refuge while the PCs occupy everyone's attention. **He shows up three turns later**, once he is satisfied.
- **If Ranza and Slate attack by surprise, Slate casts *heartseeker* on Ranza's wooden daggers**, which Ranza then uses in backstabbing attempts.
- Thief abilities: PP 60%, OL 60%, F/RT 60%, MS 35%, HS 95%, DN 95%, CW 95%. **Backstab for quadruple damage.**
- Wild talents: psychic impersonation, probe, ESP contact. 142 PSPs.
- His room contains a **decoy chest** — locked, holding 107 cp, with the key hidden in a groove under the table in his parlour. **Hidden under a loose stone in the floor** is loot kept from his thieving days: a dark ruby (75 gp), a large emerald (100 gp), and three blue quartz crystals (75 cp each).
- Like the rest of the Square, he is **resistant to mind reading** and was recruited via charm, initiated by Gurdek, and left insane but with his identity and memories intact.

## GM-Only Secrets
- **He is the module's designated defection, and it is signposted in the text.** *"If the PCs identify him as the least loyal member of the Square, they may be able to get favours from him, ranging from helpful hints to outright betrayal."* This is the intended solution to the Refuge, and it is available to any party that watches the group for ten minutes and notices which one is unhappy.
- **The tell is the clumsiness.** He moves badly on purpose. A thief PC, or anyone who has ever watched a professional, should be allowed to notice that a man this awkward is working at it.
- **He does not know he wants out.** Do not play him as a man waiting to be recruited. Play him as a man in a bad mood who becomes fractionally more alive every time someone treats him as what he used to be. The turn should feel like something the players caused rather than something he was holding ready.
- **He is the most dangerous individual in the Refuge in a straight fight** — quadruple backstab, 95% hide, and Slate casting heartseeker onto his daggers. If the party fails to identify him and the Square ambushes them, Ranza is what kills someone.
- *(Proposed.)* The stash under the floorboard is the tell he keeps from himself. A holy man with no status and no wealth does not need a ruby hidden under a stone.

## Proposed Developments
- **Recommended:** make the defection winnable and make it cost something. He will not fight the Square for the party, but he will look the other way, mislay a password, or point at the basement.
- If the party kills the Square without ever noticing him, that is a legitimate outcome — and the GM should let them find the ruby under the floor afterwards and work out what they missed.
- If he defects and survives, a 12th-level thief who has lost his vocation twice is a genuinely interesting recurring NPC for the underground adventures.

- **Resistant to mind reading:** Intelligence check to read him at all; natural 20 dazes the reader for 1d6 rounds.
- **Running him at the table:** sour, unremarkable, and standing slightly too far from the group. Give him one line of unprompted praise for Slate and one flat refusal to discuss the gith, and let attentive players do the rest.
