The band's second weaponsmith — the one who makes the spears and knives while Tenpug makes the unusual pieces — and the only person in the war debate proposing an actual tactic rather than a mood.

## Player-Safe Description

### Appearance
*(Proposed.)* Burn-marked forearms, a smith's shoulders, and the permanent squint of someone who works next to an oven. Works in the baker's tent because that is where the heat is — the band fires its pottery and forges its weapons off Roi's ovens, which means Thren spends his days in the warmest and best-smelling room in the temple.

### Manner and Voice
*(Proposed, extrapolated from his printed argument.)* Practical and unhurried, with the specific confidence of a man who is good at one thing and knows exactly how far that competence extends. Thren talks about the war the way he talks about a commission: what have we got, what can we make, how long have we got.

He is the only debater on either side who proposes a method.

### Public Reputation
Solid. Not a leader, not a talker, and the second-most important person in the camp the moment the band decides to fight.

## Confirmed Facts
- A talented weaponsmith of Tenpug's Band. He and Tenpug both work in the baker's tent, using the ovens' heat.
- **Thren mostly makes spears and small knives.** Tenpug's greater skill is reserved for more unusual pieces. This division of labour is printed.
- A minor debater in the second meeting tent, arguing **for war**.
- His proposal: force the gith to besiege the temple, while the PCs fight them a few at a time.

## Resources and Capabilities
- **Weapon production.** With Tenpug, he is the reason the band can arm itself at all. Spears and knives in quantity is exactly what a levy of craftsmen needs.
- Repair capability — the module notes roughly 10% of bone weapons and 20% of stone weapons are damaged after the first battle, and that Tenpug can repair many given two or three days. Thren is the other half of that capacity.
- Working knowledge of what can be made from what: obsidian from the quarry, bone from the wastes, sink-worm teeth from the silt, and captured gith steel.
- A siege plan that is more coherent than anything else proposed in either tent.

## Current Activity
Making spears, as fast as materials allow, regardless of which way the vote goes.

## GM-Only Secrets
- **Thren is the module's forgotten logistics NPC.** Adventure One's "Forging Weapons" section (p.30) makes weapon quality and quantity the band's worst problem and routes every solution — the obsidian quarry, the sink worm, raiding the gith supply tents — through Tenpug alone. Thren doubles that capacity and the text never says so. A GM running the material-gathering side quests should have him in the room.
- **His siege plan is half right.** Forcing the gith to besiege the temple is sound; the assumption that the PCs can then whittle them down a few at a time is not, because the gith answer to a closed gate is to light a fire in front of it and smoke everyone out. That is printed. Thren has not thought about smoke.
- *(Proposed.)* He is the natural person to take over arming the band if Tenpug is killed or absent — which, from Adventure Two onward, Tenpug increasingly is.

## Proposed Developments
- **Recommended:** attach Thren to any weapon-material expedition as the NPC who tells the party what is worth carrying back. The obsidian quarry and the sink-worm carcass both need someone to say *this makes a spear and that does not*.
- Give him the smoke problem in council. A craftsman working next to a bread oven all day is well placed to be the one who realises what the gith will do, and to be ignored twice in one week.
- If Adventure Two takes Tenpug away with the army, Thren inherits the forge and the camp's entire production. That is a promotion the campaign gets for free.
