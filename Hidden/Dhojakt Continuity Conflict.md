# Historical Record: Dhojakt — who transformed Nibenay's son

## Subject and Period

- Subject: the transformation of **Dhojakt** (`actor-dhojakt`) into a being half man, half cilops — and who caused it
- Exact day or approximate period: the event itself is placed in his infancy, with no confirmed day. The conflict was identified on **day 116** (s3 ep.20), when Kantim told his own version to Utopi
- Historical status: disputed

*Identified during the mk-sandbox cross-check of the summary `s3 ep.20 - Rivers of Blood` (Markon), 2026-08-06.*

## Account

Dhojakt is the son of Nibenay, transformed into a unique being that is half man and half cilops. Who did it to him is not agreed: the recorded lore attributes the transformation to his mother (or to the Pristine Tower), while what was said at the table on day 116 attributes the act to the father himself, with the mother resisting.

## Affected Elements

- Actors: `actor-dhojakt`; `actor-nibenay` (the Sorcerer King as possible perpetrator); Dhojakt's mother (concubine, unnamed in the sources); the eunuch **Kantim** (source of the table version); **Utopi** (the PC who heard it)
- Locations: Nibenay; the Pristine Tower (as the alternative cause in the existing rumor)
- Records: `mk-repos/mk-sandbox/actors/dhojakt.json` (revision 4, read-only); `Περίληψη - s3 ep.20 - Rivers of Blood.md`

## Consequences Still Present

Nibenay's character changes depending on the version: in one he is a father who inherited a disaster, in the other he is the one who deliberately inflicted it on his own infant. Whichever holds also determines how Dhojakt's withdrawal from the city is read today.

## Conflicting Accounts

**mk-sandbox — `actor-dhojakt`, revision 4:**

> *"Nibenay's son, transformed into a unique being that is half man and half cilops. **His mother is believed to have used spells to alter his body** in an attempt to make him more powerful. A **competing rumor** claims that he **ventured too close to the Pristine Tower** and was transformed into the first member of an entirely new race."*

**Table — ep.20, day 116, the GM narrating what Utopi knows from the eunuch Kantim:**

> **Nibenay** was not pleased with the child; he considered its potential wasted and **turned his magic on the infant**, again and again, until it was warped into a beast. **His mother did not want to give him that control.**

The detail is not what differs — **the perpetrator differs**. In the sandbox the mother is the cause (or the Pristine Tower); at the table the mother is the one who resisted, and the perpetrator is the father.

### Check against the printed books (2026-08-09)

The Dark Sun Wiki cites three books as sources for Nibenay. All three exist locally as markdown. None mentions the transformation:

| Book (local .md) | "Dhojakt" | "cilops" | "Pristine Tower" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marauders of Nibenay | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| The Ivory Triangle | 1 (p. 158) | 29 | 0 |
| City by the Silt Sea | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The **only** printed text we hold is *The Ivory Triangle* p. 158: half man, half cilops, recognised as Prince of Nibenay, recently withdrawn from public view after driving a defiler from the city. **It is silent on how it happened.**

The "mother / Pristine Tower" origin appears on no printed page in the corpus — it is wiki-level lore. That is precisely why the sandbox sourceRefs admits *"exact published book and page remain to be attached."*

### The online versions

> **Dark Sun Wiki, Nibenay (Sorcerer-King):** *"Nibenay has a son named Dhojakt, who has a monstrous form. It is believed his mother, in order to make him more powerful, used spells to modify his body. It is also rumored that his son got too close to the Pristine Tower, causing him to transform into a new race."*
>
> Elsewhere the same page hedges harder: *"his son has become a half-cilops abomination through unknown means."*

The sandbox's `actor-dhojakt` copies that wording almost verbatim.

Everything found, side by side:

**No source anywhere states it as fact.** Every version is a rumor layer — exactly the shape the table version fits into.

One further point in favour: the other group's campaign wiki writes *"He held a position of respect in the Stone City. Especially since Father did not make many personal appearances"* — which agrees with The Ivory Triangle p. 158 (recognised Prince, the king seldom seen). The rest of that page (Dhojakt killed by the Warbringers in the Glowing Desert, a spell list) is their table, **not canon** — our Dhojakt is alive.

## Player-Safe Version

Conflicting rumors circulate about Dhojakt: that his mother transformed him with spells to make him more powerful; that he came too close to the Pristine Tower; and — spoken low inside the palace — that his own father did it. None is publicly confirmed.

## GM-Only Notes

Options, **all untested and unapproved**:

1. **Leave it open.** The two versions coexist as conflicting rumors — which the sandbox record already does, since it contains a "competing rumor". The table version simply becomes a **third rumor**. *Recommended by the author of this note* — cheapest, and it fits how the record is already written.
2. **The table version becomes the truth.** The other two are the cover story the palace spread. Dramatically the strongest: it explains why Dhojakt is hidden and why no one speaks of him.
3. ~~**The sandbox is right and Kantim is wrong.**~~ **DEAD** after the 2026-08-09 check. There is no printed authority to defer to: the sandbox version is copied from a wiki, without a page, and the three books the wiki cites do not contain it. On this point the sandbox carries no more weight than the table.

The sandbox's source is admittedly incomplete — *"exact published book and page remain to be attached."* **It was searched for and not found** (see "Check against the printed books"). The question cannot be settled by text; it needs a campaign owner decision between options 1 and 2.
