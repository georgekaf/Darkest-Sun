# Below Kharanok — Athasian Names & Lore

Alternate naming pass over [[Below Kharanok]], reflavoring the generic Dyson MegaDelve names into something native to Athas — defiling scars, sorcerer-king-era metal scarcity, elemental cult, psionics, the Sea of Silt. Numbering (01–31) and floor grouping are unchanged from the base index; only names and flavor text are new here. [[05. The Mushroom Cavern|Mushroom Cavern]]/[[30. Under the Mushroom Cave|Under the Mushroom Cave]], [[06. Lost River Cave (East)|Lost River Cave]] (East/West/South), [[10. Under the Lost River Temple|Under the Lost River Temple]], [[22. Lost River|Lost River]], and [[24. The Water Temple|Water Temple]] keep their names as-is — water and fungus are precious enough on Athas that plain description already carries weight — but get expanded lore below.

**Frame:** halls 11–17 and 31 are reframed as **Kharun-Thal**, a dwarven Blue Age city — built before the world's defiling collapsed it into desert, when metal was common instead of a fortune in coin. Its wealth is why the halls are still worth dying for.

## Floor 1

**01 — The Glass Maw** *(was: Obsidian Mines AKA The Cannibal Caves)*
A volcanic vent glassed to black mirror-stone millennia ago. The obsidian vein draws honest miners from Kharanok, but the deeper tunnels belong to a nest of black mastyrials — pack-hunting, pincered burrowers that share a crude group mind and go quiet, all at once, right before they charge.

## Floor 2

**02 — The Gnawing Warrens** *(was: The Rat-Folk Tunnels)*
A cilops warren, burrowed between levels — the centipedes never sleep and never stop tracking, hunting in relays until whatever they've scented drops. Dwarves call the ceaseless clicking through the walls an omen and don't go down without leaving an offering.

**03 — The Bone Den** *(was: The Ogre Base)*
A boneclaw's lair, floor deep in the cracked remains of everything it's run down over the years. It charges anything that moves in a straight line, fast enough that most never see past its bone-plated head before impact.

**04 — The Chittering Hollow** *(was: The Goblin Caves)*
A slig nest, named for the constant dry rattle of them moving in the dark. They raid the [[05. The Mushroom Cavern|Mushroom Cavern]] in packs, flanking anything that stops to fight the ones out front.

**05 — The Mushroom Cavern** *(name kept)*
A hot-spring cavern gone to fungus, the single most valuable food source under the mountain. Old miners swear the fungus itself dreams — that the caps grow toward footsteps, not water — and refuse to farm it after dark.

**06 — Lost River Cave (East)** *(name kept)*
Where the underground river surfaces first, cold and mineral-sweet. A shrine of river-smoothed stones sits half-submerged at the ford — no one living built it, and no one living has moved a stone from it either.

**07 — Lost River Cave (West)** *(name kept)*
The river slows and pools here, wide enough that something in it has grown large on the traffic between levels. Boatless crossing is a fool's option; most parties that haven't lost anyone yet, lose someone here.

**08 — The Blind Reach** *(was: The Morlock Retreat)*
A mastyrial colony gone pale from generations without light, hunting entirely by the click and echo of their own calls. They freeze at any sound louder than dripping water — and unfreeze all at once, from every direction.

**09 — Lost River Cave (South)** *(name kept)*
The river forks here — one branch toward the temple below, one into a passage crushed flat by a rockfall no crew has ever cleared. Whether the collapse was natural or not, nothing has un-dammed it since.

## Floor 3

**30 — Under the Mushroom Cave** *(name kept)*
The Cavern's root system, dense and phosphorescent, threaded through with an old shaft down to the Bloodstone Deeps. Miners who cut too deep into the fungus mass sometimes don't come back with the same face they went down with.

## Floor 4

**10 — Under the Lost River Temple** *(name kept)*
A drowned shrine to water itself, from an age before Kharanok rationed every cup. Water-priests who find it treat it as holy ground reclaimed; everyone else treats it as a very deep, very cold place to die.

## Floor 5

**11 — The Starmetal Vault** *(was: The Mithril Hall)*
Named for the fallen-star iron the dwarves of Kharun-Thal once worked here — metal that didn't rust, didn't dull, and fell from the sky often enough to build a hall around the forging of it. On Athas today a shaving of it would buy a caravan.

**12 — The Sealed Gate of Kharun-Thal** *(was: Gates of the City)*
The dwarven city's formal threshold, staircase up to the [[31. The Harpy Tower|Aerie of Broken Wings]] collapsed generations ago per the old records. Whatever sealed it did so from the inside.

**31 — The Aerie of Broken Wings** *(was: The Harpy Tower)*
A wind-scoured spire above Kharun-Thal's gate, nested by a kes'trekel flock long after the dwarves stopped climbing to it. See [[12. Gates of the City|The Sealed Gate of Kharun-Thal]] — the stair between them is rubble, not route.

## Floor 6

**13 — The Bronze Concourse** *(was: The Hall of Bronze)*
Kharun-Thal's main junction, a cylindrical stair dropping and rising to half the city at once. The bronze facing has gone green-black with age but still rings true when struck — the dwarves built it to be heard from every level, and it still can be.

**14 — The Petrified Court** *(was: The Marble Hall)*
A grand hall quarried, not built — the dwarves carved the mountain's own stone into columns rather than haul marble from a surface that no longer exists for them. Stairs up connect it to [[03. The Ogre Base|The Bone Den]]; so far the boneclaw hasn't found the passage, and everyone would like to keep it that way.

**15 — The Silent Barrows of Kharun-Thal** *(was: Dwarven Crypts)*
Where the city buried its focus-keepers — dwarves whose life's singular purpose was the city itself, laid to rest under their own unfinished work. The bridge from the Petrified Court collapsed long before anyone alive today was born; nothing here has been disturbed since.

## Floor 7

**16 — The Rust Halls** *(was: The Iron Halls)*
Corroded to lace in most places, iron this old shouldn't still hold weight — and mostly doesn't. What was once the city's arsenal is now mostly a warning about what happens to wealth nobody living remembers how to maintain.

**17 — The Weeping Vault** *(was: Venomous Hall)*
Something in the old ventilation shafts sweats a venom that beads on the walls like condensation. The stair down to the [[26. The Hematite Mines|Bloodstone Deeps]] reeks of it the whole way.

## Floor 8

**18 — The Drowned Adit** *(was: The Flooded Mines)*
A mine shaft the water table reclaimed, now the low point every other flooded passage on this floor eventually drains toward. Whatever the dwarves were digging for down here, it's underwater and has been for longer than Kharun-Thal has been a ruin.

**19 — The Bone Gardens of Ur-Kesh** *(was: The Necropolis of Bryn Mynnyd)*
Row on row of the dead, laid out with a care that suggests whoever built this necropolis expected visitors, or return. Black silt horrors work the graves now, dragging fresh remains under the silt-damp floor faster than anything can be recovered. The surface exit here doesn't lead back to the Blackspine range at all — it comes up in Quor'Anok.

**20 — The Withered Choir** *(was: The Death Cult)*
A sand howler pack denned in the hollow next door, their call carrying through the stone into the Bone Gardens like a chorus that never learned to stop. Nothing living has worked out whether they're mourning or hunting.

**21 — The Salt Crypts** *(was: The Crypts)*
Bodies here weren't embalmed so much as they were salted, the way Athas preserves everything worth keeping. The salt bleeding down from somewhere above has kept the dead recognizable for far longer than flesh should last.

**22 — Lost River** *(name kept)*
The river's final open stretch before it vanishes into the Silt Wallow. Cleaner water than anywhere else on this floor, which is exactly why nothing that lives down here will admit to needing it.

**23 — The Silt Wallow** *(was: Home of the Muck Dwellers)*
A mud-flat basin where the river gives up and spreads thin — home to a silt horror that lies still as sediment until something wades close enough to lure in. A small, ugly, unlucky echo of the Sea of Silt above.

**24 — The Water Temple** *(name kept)*
Sacred beyond argument to anyone who's rationed a cup of water their whole life. Whatever lives in the deep pool below the altar surfaces rarely enough that no one's had to test whether it's a guardian or just the largest thing that's ever wanted the water for itself.

**25 — The Stormvein Mines** *(was: The Thunder Mines)*
Named for fulgurite glass, veins of stone fused by lightning strikes so old the storms that made them predate the desert itself. Rare enough on the surface to be sacred; down here it's just ore, if you can stand what guards it.

**26 — The Bloodstone Deeps** *(was: The Hematite Mines)*
Hematite ore, blood-red when wet, worked by a chain of connected shafts to three other levels. The dwarves called it ill-luck stone and mined it anyway — Kharun-Thal needed the iron more than it feared the omen.

**27 — The Kragfall Shaft** *(was: Dwarven Outpost)*
An old dwarven guardpost, empty of dwarves for longer than anyone's kept count — claimed instead by a krag that's worked out the shaft up to [[30. Under the Mushroom Cave|Under the Mushroom Cave]] makes for the best ambush choke point on the floor, and defends it like the territory it now is.

**28 — The Citadel of Cracked Bone** *(was: Giant Citadel – North)*
A fortress built to a scale no living thing on this floor needs — halls big enough that a tarek can turn around in them without touching a wall, which is exactly what's nested here now. The bones littering the floor are too numerous, and too varied, to belong to one meal.

**29 — The Citadel of Ash** *(was: Giant Citadel – South)*
Twin to the northern hold, burned out at some point by a fire that shouldn't have found enough fuel down here to burn this hot. A gorak herd has moved into the wreckage since, docile in numbers, dangerous the moment anything spooks the lead bull.
