# Dorak

**Role:** Ex-mine foreman — keeper of the oldest and most dangerous knowledge in Kharanok
**Found at:** [[Old Quarry Yard]]
**Faction:** Kharanok Local

---

## Background

Thirty years ago, Dorak's crew broke through the wrong wall in the lowest quarry cut. What was on the other side didn't come through — but it communicated. The sealing took three days. He has never spoken about what he heard. He supervised the construction of Kharanok's foundations afterward, partly to stay close to the sealed entrance, partly because keeping busy stopped him thinking. He has kept the sealing instructions scratched on a flat stone hidden in the quarry yard for three decades, waiting for a day he hoped would never come. He knows the village was built on top of something very old and very patient. He knows the entity behind the door is intelligent — it found Oren, worked through intermediaries to chip the exterior markings, tested the seal from both sides simultaneously. Dorak has been watching it work and saying nothing, because there was nothing to say until someone capable of listening arrived.

---

## Agenda

Keep the seal intact. Provide what the players need to reinforce it — without going near it himself. Pass on what he knows in fragments, because giving it all at once would mean fully acknowledging what he has lived with for thirty years.

---

## Wants & Needs

- **Wants:** To not die knowing the seal will fail after him
- **Needs:** To actually tell someone the full story before it is too late — the weight of carrying it alone has become its own kind of damage

---

## Goals

- Seal reinforced and stable, with someone else holding the knowledge
- The hidden second pen disclosed and used — it is the last practical thing he can leave behind
- The clay tablet translated and understood by someone who will take it seriously

---

## Roleplay Notes

Long silences before answers. Doesn't lie, but frequently says nothing instead of responding. Will hand over the sealing-stone rather than describe it. When something connects to his fear — showing him the bridge symbol, asking about the sound behind the wall — he goes quiet and the silence lasts minutes. Not evasive about ordinary inconvenient things; only about things he is genuinely afraid of. The instructions *"Do not bring light. Do not speak names. Do not look at what stands behind the door"* are delivered without drama, as instructions rather than as horror. When he finally shares something he has kept for thirty years, he says it quickly, flatly, and then changes the subject.
