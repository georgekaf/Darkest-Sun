# Sira

**Role:** Head herder — mountain trail specialist, field intelligence
**Found at:** [[Hidden/Kharanok- The Altar of Dust/Pens]]
**Faction:** Kharanok Local

---

## Background

Sira has worked the pens since she was seven. She knows the Black Spine the way most people know the inside of their own house — the specific trails, the seasonal animal patterns, the ground that goes soft after rain, the sound of the mountains at night. For weeks she has been tracking something wrong: dust trails that don't match weather, claw marks at the wrong height, a dry-wash gully that something has been using near the sealed cave entrance. She reported it to Elder Yethras and was dismissed. She now keeps precise written records in a leather folio she carries everywhere, because she learned early that people argue with memory but can't argue with paper. She has been right about everything. She is tired of waiting for someone to listen.

---

## Agenda

Get someone to take her records seriously — on the first telling, not after being proven right. Scout the marked locations. Stop whatever is getting into the pens and killing the animals. Restock the village before it starves.

---

## Wants & Needs

- **Wants:** To be taken seriously without having to prove it first
- **Needs:** Authority that matches her expertise — she knows more about the mountain than anyone alive in Kharanok, and she has no formal power to act on it

---

## Goals

- Pens fully restocked and self-sustaining
- Underground intrusion into the gorge identified and stopped
- Secondary spring located and reconnected (her grandmother used it — she knows approximately where it was)

---

## Roleplay Notes

Blunt to the point of seeming rude, but not aggressive — she just doesn't see the value in softening things. Answers questions precisely and literally. If you ask whether she's seen anything strange near the quarry, she will describe three specific events, in order, with approximate dates. Does not speculate. If she doesn't know something, she says so immediately and names who does. Her folio is physical — she produces it; she doesn't narrate it. Softens slightly and visibly around the animals. The line *"I don't know what it is. But it goes to the sealed door and comes back. And sometimes it doesn't come alone"* is delivered flat, because she has already accepted the implications and moved on to the problem of what to do about it.
