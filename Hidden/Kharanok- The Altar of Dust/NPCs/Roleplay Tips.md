# Roleplay Tips — Kharanok NPCs

General guidance for running Kharanok's cast at the table.

---

## The Village Tone

Kharanok is not cheerful. It is a settlement at the end of a long, slow emergency. The people here are exhausted, not broken — there is a difference. They are still functioning, still doing their jobs, but the effort it costs is visible. Let that weight read in every NPC. Even moments of progress should feel like hard-won ground, not relief.

---

## Information as Currency

Every local NPC knows something the players don't, and most don't lead with it. Let players ask. The asking tells the NPC whether these are people worth trusting. Yethras won't volunteer that the council is fractured. Dorak won't open with thirty years of suppressed knowledge. Sira will share everything — but only if you sit down and take the folio seriously first.

**Principle:** slow reveals over fast exposition. Information that costs something to get is information players will remember.

---

## Every NPC Has a Tell

Give players something to notice and reward them when they do.

- **Yethras** restates what you said before responding — checking it was actually said
- **Breck** speaks in shorter sentences when tired; at his best he's terse, at his worst he's monosyllabic
- **Sira** produces the folio physically rather than narrating from memory
- **Dorak** goes quiet on specific topics — not all topics, only the ones that matter
- **Kallia Yelka** never stands at the south end of the cistern
- **Oren** asks a question back when he wants to change the subject

When players notice these and act on them, they're doing good roleplay. Reward it with something real.

---

## Calibration NPCs

Different NPCs work well at different moments in the session.

**Sira** is your precision instrument — literal, data-forward, no embellishment. When players are lost in vagueness, send them to the pens.

**Breck** is your tension gauge — he will tell players exactly how bad things are without drama. Use him to recalibrate when the session is drifting.

**Yethras** is your stakes anchor — his exhaustion reflects the village's condition. If he seems lighter, things are improving. If he seems heavier, the players feel it.

---

## Let NPCs Evaluate Each Other

These people know each other and have opinions. Use those opinions as hooks.

- Breck respects Sira and will say so, bluntly: *"She's been right about everything."*
- Sira thinks Yethras is a coward — she won't say "coward," she'll say *"He already knew. He just didn't act."*
- Dorak trusts nobody easily, but will volunteer one unprompted positive thing about a player who has helped him: a single sentence, once, not repeated
- Kallia Yelka respects efficiency above everything and will acknowledge it in anyone — including players who handle her removal cleanly

These are not neutral observations. They are hooks and complications.

---

## The Traitor and the Frightened

**Oren** can be more interesting than a straightforward arrest. If players decide to use him rather than arrest him, he is a motivated, frightened, useful asset. He has the handler's name, the merchant house connection, and a strong survival instinct. Play that honestly — he is not brave or heroic, but he is functional when he has something to lose.

**Kallia Yelka** is not a villain. She found something terrifying, made bad choices, and has been alone with the consequences for three months. If players handle her exposure with care rather than publicly destroying her, she will cooperate fully with the cistern restoration. Her knowledge of the lower vault is operationally important; her emotional state is just a person who made frightened choices and knows it.

---

## The Thing Below

The entity behind the [[Hidden/Kharanok- The Altar of Dust/Lower Caves (Sealed)|Lower Cave seal]] is intelligent, patient, and very old. If players interact with the sealed entrance, it communicates — it does not threaten. The horror is not that it will attack. It's that it has been waiting, with full knowledge of how long it has been waiting, and it still has time. Play its communications as calm, certain, and deeply unhurried. It does not need to rush.

---

## The Outsiders

**Jessix** names his price first, before you can offer. That's not greed — it's learned self-protection. His asks are always reasonable, which is what makes them interesting. His attention sharpening when the Lower Caves come up is the tell that this is not purely transactional for him.

**Muuton** decides quickly when he has enough data and will tell you when he needs more. Respect it. He is not stalling; he is being honest about his process. Don't play him as diplomatic — he is direct, and he finds diplomacy inefficient.

**Jessareen** already knows some of what players found. When she asks about the passes, she is checking whether they're telling the truth. If they are, something shifts in her — slightly, noticeably — in the direction of someone who just learned they are not alone with something very large.

---

## Pacing Note

The quest chain is designed so that each set's revelations make the next set feel inevitable. When NPCs share information, connect it forward: *"That pass movement you found — that's what Jessix has been watching from the other side."* Let the players feel the net tightening. The final reveal — that the merchant house, the gith, the seal, and the psionic buyer are all threads in the same cloth — should feel earned, not surprising.
