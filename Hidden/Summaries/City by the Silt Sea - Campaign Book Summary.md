# City by the Silt Sea — Campaign Book Narrative Summary

## Chapter 1: History of Giustenal

Giustenal was one of the seven ancient city-states of Athas, ruled by Dregoth the Dread King. Unlike the other sorcerer-kings, Dregoth was murdered 2,000 years ago — ambushed in his own palace by seven fellow sorcerer-kings who feared his growing power and the madness that consumed Borys (the Dragon of Athas). The assault destroyed the city: defiling magic erupted tar pits from the earth, the northern half collapsed into the Silt Sea (then still water), and thousands died instantly.

Dregoth's survivors fled underground. He returned as an undead dragon king (kaisharga), leading his people to a cavern prepared during the Blue Age for halfling settlement. There he founded New Giustenal and began the experiments that created the dray — a draconic humanoid race.

## Chapter 2: The Silt Sea Environs

The region around Giustenal is a blasted wasteland of tar pits, silt shallows, and ancient ruins. The Sea of Silt forms the northern border — a vast expanse of choking gray dust that was once a real sea before defiling magic boiled it away. The tar pits to the south are smoking, noxious obstacles that require survival checks to cross (with risks: missteps, blindness, heat exhaustion, pit snatchers).

Key locations:
- **Black Spine Mountains** — volcanic range to the east, source of the Blackjaw River
- **Break Shore** — the silt-covered northern edge of the ruins
- **The Shifting Slope** — unstable terrain south of Giustenal, collapsing sinkholes
- **Bodach** — a ruined outpost village
- **The Silt Road** — ancient trade route now buried

Random encounter tables cover silt horrors, silt serpents, gith raiders, elf traders, and the ever-present environmental hazards.

## Chapter 3: Island Settlements

The Silt Sea islands harbor scattered communities:

- **Sky Singers** — an elf tribe led by Jessareen, who has gone mad from the Caller in Darkness after losing her lover Slinnasia to Giustenal's ruins. They operate the Sky Singers' Market — a mobile bazaar on the trade roads. Jessix the Wanderer, a former Sky Singer, seeks to help his people.

- **House M'ke Village** — one of the two merchant houses operating in the region. They control silt piers and trade routes. Pier of House M'ke is a fortified docking point on the Silt Sea's edge.

- **Pirate Island** — base for silt pirates who raid caravans and boats. The Fire Dagger clan (elves) are among the most feared raiders here.

- **Sharg Island** — uninhabited, named after the creature that lurks nearby.

- **Island Settlements** — small independent fishing and trading outposts.

## Chapter 4: Cromlin

Cromlin is a trade town on the edge of the Silt Sea, technically under Nibenay's control but practically independent. It's a lawless, dusty settlement where merchant houses, templars, and criminals intersect.

**Factions:**
- **House Shom** — the dominant merchant house. Operates the Pier of House Shom, a Lodge (luxury inn/meeting hall), a Barracks (mercenaries), and a Warehouse (goods storage). They trade in metal, weapons, and information.
- **House M'ke** — rival house, less established in Cromlin but aggressive.
- **Nibenese Templars** — Nibenay's agents, corrupt and extractive.
- **Fire Dagger Clan** — elf assassins and raiders, operating from the shadows. Hired for dirty work.
- **Lodgden** — a rogue/thief working both sides.

**Key Locations:**
- **Central Gates** — heavily guarded entry
- **Sky Singers' Market** — elf trade bazaar
- **Happy Hurrum Tavern** — rowdy commoner bar
- **Dirty Lizard** — seedy criminal tavern
- **Beetle's Bite** — a tavern (shared name with New Giustenal's rival bar)
- **Silt Piers** — where silt skimmers dock
- **Common Section, Fishing Shacks, Fanner Fields** — working-class areas

**Plots:** House rivalry over trade routes, Fire Dagger assassinations, templar corruption, silt monster attacks.

## Chapter 5: The Ruins Below

### The Groaning City

The uppermost cavern beneath Giustenal where demihumans (dwarves, elves, halflings) fled during the Cleansing Wars. Dregoth's troops found and massacred them. The dwarves were hanged from trees, cursed by Dregoth to become undead — their moans echo through the cavern. Key locations: the Ancient Temple, Tunnels Beneath Giustenal, the Citadel, and Taraskir's Tomb (a demihuman hero buried here with his magical spear Fang of the Lion).

### Kragmorta

A fiery cavern inhabited by the **first generation dray** — flawed mutants rejected by Dregoth. Led by Mosak (Clan Father), 324 dray live in a settlement of ancient buildings, constantly threatened by predators Dregoth releases into the cavern for study or amusement: wall-walkers (hive-mind insectoid hunters), hell hounds, fire drakes, dark nagas, and a spirit entity.

Key locations:
- Entrance cavern with sheer 100-foot drop
- Lava river crossing
- Dray settlement (ancient buildings repurposed)
- Dark naga lair (contains book of vile darkness, net of snaring)
- Fire drake island (25-foot dragon, hunts on surface through lava tube)
- Wall-walker hive (24 creatures, compete with kalin)
- Fresh water springs (guarded by dray)
- Ancient Temple (halfling-era structure)
- **Passk's Shack** — Passk is a first-gen dray sage who knows the tunnels and can guide PCs. He's searching for the lost Fang of the Lion spear.
- **Exit to surface** — a secret lava tube to the Black Spine Mountains

### New Giustenal

Dregoth's underground city, built from dragon bones. Population ~3,000 second-generation dray. Illuminated by continual light on stalactites. Two rivers (Blackjaw and Little Blackjaw) provide water. Society is ordered, militarized, and devout.

**14 Key Locations:**
1. **Central Gates** — twin towers, bone golems, 8 templars
2. **Temple of the Dragon** — 3 temples, Mon Adderath and Absalom preach Dregoth's divinity. Blood sacrifices on obsidian altars.
3. **Blackjaw River** — steaming water over magma, fishing barges. Sharg threatens. Dregoth's undead army hidden in a cave east along the river.
4. **Sharg Island** — abandoned estate, sharg lairs beneath. Dregoth studies it for control.
5. **Fishing Shacks** — dray fishermen worried about the sharg. No sailing vessels — barges dragged with nets.
6. **Fanner Fields** — cattle-like beasts called fanners raised for meat
7. **Fungi Fields** — cultivated mushrooms (supplement diet, also used for munge drink)
8. **Common Section** — simple homes for common dray around the central pillar
9. **Dread Palace** — black obsidian, dragon bone decorations, planar gate inside. Detailed in adventure book.
10. **Upside-Down Forest** — Blue Age remnant, trees grow from ceiling, fruit is a dray staple
11. **Akrag's Pools** — public bath run by Akrag (secretly Absalom in disguise). Known for assassinations.
12. **The Pit** — tavern run by Freiha and Gatlakk. Famous for munge (mushroom liquor). Rival Casskka of Beetle's Bite hired thief Lodgden to steal the recipe.
13. **College of Blackspire** — mage academy. Brainwashing, psionic screening. Produces loyal defilers.
14. **Beetle's Bite Tavern** — rival to The Pit, failing business, cheap blackjaw fish

**Dregoth's Templars:** 338 templars + 100 kalin riders. Scaled ranking system (First Scale = 3rd level, up to Mon Adderath at Highest Scale/18th). Militia system — every able dray expected to fight when Dregoth marches.

**Kalin Riders:** Elite templars riding insectoid mounts. 4 squadrons of 25. Patrol ceilings, lead surface assault. Each squadron has an attached defiler (7th-10th level).

## Chapter 6: Dregoth and the Dray

### Dregoth

- 29th-level kaisharga (undead dragon king)
- 30 feet long, 20,000 lbs
- AC -8, MR 40%, 173 hp
- Full psionics (330 PSPs, all disciplines), wizard spells up to 10th level
- Breath weapon: 20d12, 50 ft long cone
- Cannot be turned
- Wears illusory forms: regal dray (common) or living dragon (rare)
- True form: damaged corpse with exposed bone, green burning eyes

**Plan for Godhood:** Dregoth discovered the planar gate in the Groaning City during life. After undeath, he traveled the planes and learned about true gods. He believes he can become Athas's first god through:
1. Mass worship (dray revere him)
2. Blood sacrifice (Raam's population targeted — prophecy of the Coruscation/Day of Light)
3. A holy relic (dragon skull with obsidian eyes, kept in his chambers)

This is impossible — Athas has no conduits for divine power. Dregoth doesn't know.

### The Dray

**First Generation:** Failed early experiments. Mutated — bent spines, jagged claws, mottled scales. Banished to Kragmorta. Still worship Dregoth but hate him. Led by Mosak. Can breed, age naturally (~70 year max). +2 Str, +2 Con, -2 Int, -1 Wis. Infravision 60 ft.

**Second Generation:** Successful transformation. Tall, regal, perfect scales. Live in New Giustenal. Max age ~120 years. +1 Str, +1 Con, +1 Int, -1 Wis. Infravision 30 ft. Believe themselves chosen people. Hate demihumans, pity humans (who they think need transformation).

Both types: AC 7 (natural), half fire damage, double cold damage, -2 attack in bright light. Speak ancient Giustenal tongue.
