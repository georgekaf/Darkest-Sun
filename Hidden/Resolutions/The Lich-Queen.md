## 3. "The meaning of the Blind Dark and whether the lich-queen is Gith are unresolved."

**Two questions again, and they part company. The Blind Dark half is partly answered — and the book tells us it is deliberately obscure.**

The phrase appears twice, both times in githyanki inscriptions:

> *"On the ornate archway that leads to there are the words, **'The Blind Dark is beneath you.'** These words are in githyanki, and even if they can be deciphered **their meaning is unclear, as this phrase refers to an obscure mystic aspect of the githyanki's adoration of their lich-queen.**"*

> *"Nlakni cast a magic mouth spell on the statue so that, should anyone touch it, it says (in githyanki), **'Beware, mortal, the wrath of the Queen. I shall bind my enemies and feed them to the Blind Dark.'** This warning represents **some insight that Nlakni had into the mysteries of the lich-queen.**"*

So the source **does** characterise it, twice over:

- it is **an obscure mystic aspect of githyanki adoration of the lich-queen** — a religious concept, not a place or a monster;
- enemies are **bound and fed to it**, which makes it a destination or a devourer in that theology;
- it is **"beneath you"** at Yathazor;
- and **Nlakni's inscription is explicitly flagged as insight into the lich-queen's mysteries** — so Nlakni is a named character who knew something about it.

**The book states outright that the meaning is unclear**, which makes the obscurity authorial intent rather than a gap. **Verdict: characterised, then deliberate silence — close it as answered-by-design, and record the two inscriptions and the Nlakni link, none of which the record carries.**

**"Whether the lich-queen is Gith"** — *(i.e. the historical Gith, the race's founder)* — **PRINTED SILENCE** in this book. Keep as its own item; it is a real question and the answer is not here.

**Proposed change:** split item 3 in two. Close the Blind Dark half with the printed characterisation; keep the identity half open, alone.
